# X2 — The exhaust talks

- **Audience:** everyone (collectors, garages, suppliers)
- **Format:** object POV. A cat-back exhaust narrates its own install day to now, deadpan and dry.
- **Runtime target:** 28.8 s (hook 0–1.2 s, body 1.2–25.4 s, CTA 25.4–28.8 s). Hard cap 30 s; W42 X2-B ran 36.8 s, so this one is cut to the bone.
- **Rhythm / device:** the exhaust is the only voice. Every beat ends on a deadpan understatement, and the comedy is the gap between a dignified voice and an indignant tailpipe. One loop: it opens "I'm Dev's exhaust" and closes "Technically, I'm Brennick's exhaust."
- **Learnings applied (2026-10-18 pre-flight diagnostics):** hook 1.2 s (W42 X2-B was 4.5 s); body line 1 at 3.2 w/s; no pause over 0.1 s before 12 s; the deadpan silence lands at 12.4 s (after 12 s); VO counted per beat; runtime capped (W42 X2-B overran by 4.8 s). Named character (Dev) at 0.5 s and car (white Corvette) at 2 s.
- **Characters and brand:** Dev, the "wrong-brand guy" and **Brennick Exhaust** are fictional. Before render, clear "Brennick" with a quick trademark and web search; swap the name if a real exhaust brand uses it. End card carries "Illustrative story. Brands and people are fictional."
- **Features shown (all live):** build page, parts registry with brand per part, log entries with dates and photos, public URL. The wrong-brand guy orders from Brennick on his own, so no shop, referral or commerce feature is shown. No (confirm) items.

## 1. Story spine
1. **Who:** a Brennick cat-back exhaust on Dev's white Corvette. It is the loudest thing he owns.
2. **Wants:** credit. Everyone loves the sound; nobody knows who made it.
3. **But:** every Sunday someone asks "What exhaust is that?" and Dev has forgotten the brand: "Uh, the loud one." A guy loudly guesses the wrong brand.
4. **Therefore:** Dev opens his build page. The parts registry shows "Cat-back exhaust · Brennick · installed Apr 6", with the install-day log entry and photos (FMB shown working, 16.0–23.3 s = 56–81 %).
5. **Payoff + loop:** the guy who guessed wrong orders one. "Technically, I'm Brennick's exhaust." That line hands the replay straight back to "I'm Dev's exhaust."

## 8. Motion notes
1. **Talking-head grammar.** The tailpipes are framed like an interview subject: centered, still, with a lower-third name chip. Stillness is the comedy; everything else in the frame can move, but the exhaust never does (except the one vapor puff at 12.4 s).
2. **The name chip is the arc.** "Exhaust · Dev's" (0 s) → "Exhaust · ???" glitch (9.0 s) → "Exhaust · Brennick" fills in from the registry (17.3 s) → "Exhaust · Brennick · Apr 6" (23.4 s). Same chip, same position, four states.
3. **Textures per beat.** Polaroid + handwrite (3.8 s), event silhouettes and bubbles (5.9 s), glitch (9.0 s), iOS build page (17.3 s), generic checkout card (20.7 s). That's a visual reset every 2–3.5 s.
4. **No real brands.** The wrong guess is a caption-redact bar. The checkout card is generic (no Shopify or Amazon look). The Corvette is shown, but no GM logo close-ups.
5. **Loop (28.8 s → 0 s).** The end frame pulls back from the same centered tailpipes as frame one, so the replay's "I'm Dev's exhaust" lands on a matching shot.
