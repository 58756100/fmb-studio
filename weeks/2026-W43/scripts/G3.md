- **Audience:** garages
- **Format:** POV skit (two voices, dialogue only, no narrator). This is the week's dialogue-vs-single-narrator test.
- **Runtime target:** 27.7 s (hook 0–1.7 s, body 1.7–24.1 s, CTA 24.1–27.7 s). Hard cap 30 s; the clock tick and typing now run under voice, which freed 1.5 s. Cut list in section 4 still applies.

- **Learnings applied (2026-10-18 pre-flight diagnostics):** hook 1.7 s; body line 1 at 3.1 w/s; the voice handoff Mia → Dale at 4.3 s is a 0.1 s gap (the beep sfx overlaps Mia's tail), so there is no dead air in 1.7–5.7 s; first deliberate break at 12.4 s (the tick and typing run under voice); VO counted per beat. - **Characters:** Mia (new tech), Dale (shop owner, out sick) and the white Integra's owner are fictional. End card carries "Illustrative story".
- **Features shown (all live):** build page, log entries with dates and photos, public URL. The URL is printed on the shop's paper work order (the shop's own habit, not a feature). No QR code, assignment, task or notification feature is shown. No (confirm) items.

## 1. Story spine
1. **Who:** Mia, a new tech on her first morning. A white Integra is on her lift.
2. **Wants:** to start the right job and not blow day one.
3. **But:** her boss Dale is out sick, and his voicemail cuts off mid-sentence: "the Integra needs—" *beep*. The owner is back at noon.
4. **Therefore:** she spots the car's link printed on the work order and opens its build page. She reads the six entries out loud, and the sixth, from August, says "rear pads, next visit" (FMB shown working, 14.5–19.6 s = 52–71 %). She starts the pads.
5. **Payoff + loop:** Dale calls back: "Rear pads? How'd you know?" Mia: "You told me. In August." The CTA "Log it for day one" loops to the hook "Day one."

## 8. Motion notes
1. **Voice = side of frame.** Mia always enters and speaks from the left, Dale from the right (voicemail card, then the incoming call). In a dialogue-only piece, viewers track who's talking by position.
2. **The cut-off (7.0 s).** The waveform flatlines with a hard red line on the beep, and the "…needs what?!" text lands on the same frame. This is the main open loop; nothing else moves for 3 frames.
3. **Paper to screen (12.0–14.4 s).** The printed URL on the work order (paper-wobble, red hand-drawn arrow) becomes the Safari bar URL via typewriter with a red caret. One continuous lens-zoom from paper into phone.
