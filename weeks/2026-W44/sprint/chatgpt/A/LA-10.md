# LA-10

**AUDIENCE:** Everyone — owners, garages and parts suppliers  
**TITLE:** Which Session Was That?  
**LENGTH:** 50 seconds. Three competing memories need a clear session-order reveal and a quieter payoff about keeping context. Approximately 2.5 spoken words per second.

## PREMISE

In a closed-course track-day paddock, a driver remembers how the car felt but the crew remembers a different order of adjustments.  
Session-labeled notes and photos on the build page reconnect each reaction to the configuration it actually describes.

## VISUAL STYLE

Vertical 9:16; asphalt, cream and signal yellow. Wide sans-serif headlines and restrained monospaced session labels. Three photographic paddock contact sheets slide across a workbench and become dated log entries. Mood: busy, then satisfyingly orderly. Reference: paddock run sheets and motorsport contact sheets, without speed worship. No live filming or subtitles. Cars remain stationary in a clearly closed-course paddock; all people, vehicles, venues and parts brands are fictional. Remove real logos.

## VOICES

One FuelMyBuild narrator, perceived age 30–45; an enthusiastic but unhurried paddock friend. Tess is the fictional driver; Sol helps in the paddock. Give **Great, except** a small smile. Emphasize **three sessions**, **sequence** and **same version**. Let the rearrangement breathe; no racing-commentator cadence. “We” is FuelMyBuild. The driver's reaction is paraphrased, not performed as a testimonial.

## CTA

**Family: DONE-FOR-YOU**  
“Comment BUILD and we'll set up your build page for you.”  
One CTA over the final paddock scene. Keyword CTA number 3 of 3 across the set; the offer means no setup on the recipient's end.

## CAPTION

“It felt better” is more useful when everyone remembers which version of the car you mean.

A fictional closed-course track day. Keep the changes, photos and people attached to the build; a log isn't telemetry.

## WHY IT HOLDS

- **Past 3 seconds:** The scrambled session cards make the memory conflict readable to anyone who's helped with a car.
- **Past 10 seconds:** A second remembered adjustment turns one confused comparison into a three-session puzzle.
- **To the end:** The physical note finally lands on the correct car configuration; the page preserves context without claiming to explain performance.
- **Comment trigger:** Owners and crews can recall forgotten changes, without racing stories or technical setup prescriptions.
