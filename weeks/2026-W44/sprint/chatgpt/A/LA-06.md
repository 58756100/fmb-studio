# LA-06

**AUDIENCE:** Garages  
**TITLE:** What That One Line Hides  
**LENGTH:** 46 seconds. A price objection opens into a physical sequence of hidden work, then returns to a fair comparison. Approximately 2.5 spoken words per second.

## PREMISE

A customer says the dealer is cheaper, while the independent shop's quote compresses a difficult repair into one ordinary-looking line.  
Photos from a similar completed build make that work understandable without pretending the record proves which quote is better.

## VISUAL STYLE

Vertical 9:16; warm gray, safety orange and rich black. Heavy grotesk headlines, light sans-serif paper labels. An accordion-fold estimate opens into photographic layers of a repair. Mood: matter-of-fact pride, no dealer-bashing. Reference: exploded technical drawings and folded shop estimates. No live filming or subtitles. Cars, garages and parts brands are fictional; no real logos. Quotes remain physical graphics outside the product.

## WHY IT HOLDS

- **Past 3 seconds:** The quoted objection is instantly recognizable to the garage audience.
- **Past 10 seconds:** The paper quote physically opens into the hidden work rather than starting a pricing lecture.
- **To the end:** The narrative returns to the original conversation with a useful visual reference, without promising a sale or justifying an unseen bill.
- **Comment trigger:** Technicians can name the difficult prep or recovery work their finished photos omit.
