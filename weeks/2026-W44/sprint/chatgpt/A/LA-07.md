# LA-07

**AUDIENCE:** Parts suppliers  
**TITLE:** Twelve Products, Zero Context  
**LENGTH:** 44 seconds. The story moves from a product-photo problem to one credible customer example, then an attainable next step; approximately 2.5 spoken words per second.

## PREMISE

A tiny parts maker has a dozen beautifully photographed products, but its most useful bracket disappears under the trunk liner once fitted.  
Instead of trying to fund ambassadors, the maker starts with a willing customer's documented installation and proper brand credit.

## VISUAL STYLE

Vertical 9:16; bright white, electric blue and workbench brown. Friendly geometric sans-serif with generous headline spacing. A pristine twelve-item catalog grid gives way to tactile customer-build photos. Mood: resourceful, specific, human. Reference: small-maker catalogs meeting an enthusiast scrapbook. No live filming or subtitles. The dozen products belong to this fictional business, not a market statistic. All brands, people and cars are invented; no real logos or testimonials.

## VOICES

One FuelMyBuild narrator, perceived age 30–40, a practical small-business ally. Read the fictional brand name as “Alder Loop.” Smile at **every angle**, emphasize **one willing owner** and **actual installation**. No invented customer endorsement or claim that the part performs well. “We” is FuelMyBuild, not Alder Loop.

## CAPTION

Your product photo shows the part. A customer's build can show where it belongs.

A fictional small maker starts with one willing owner and a documented installation. Owner permission comes before sharing.

## WHY IT HOLDS

- **Past 3 seconds:** The hidden bracket turns “customer proof” into something viewers can literally see disappear.
- **Past 10 seconds:** More product-photo angles fail to answer the placement question; viewers anticipate the missing installed view.
- **To the end:** One documented, consenting customer offers a manageable starting point for a maker without an ambassador budget.
- **Comment trigger:** Suppliers can describe useful hidden products. The story does not trace shipping, compare sponsored traffic or invent a testimonial.

## IMAGES NEEDED

- Twelve fictional, generic small automotive products on white; one is the featured Alder Loop trunk bracket. Count the grid accurately.
- Featured bracket as an isolated product, installed under a liner and covered by the liner; matching same-car photographs.
- Unbadged fictional coupe rear view, open trunk and neutral workbench.
- Simple permission-note prop, clearly editorial and outside the product interface. No people photos needed.
- Illustrative owner build page with dated installation photos, Alder Loop registry entry and installer credit. No supplier page, campaign tools, endorsement score, product analytics or automated permission feature.
