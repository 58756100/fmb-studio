# LA-02

**AUDIENCE:** Collectors  
**TITLE:** The Right Shape, the Wrong Evidence  
**LENGTH:** 50 seconds. The judge's question needs room for a false answer, a more useful piece of evidence and a qualified payoff; approximately 2.5 spoken words per second.

## PREMISE

At a fictional period-correct show, a judge questions a wheel that looks exactly right for the car.  
The owner brings an installation date, then realizes the relevant evidence is the wheel's actual identity and period reference.

Reference: museum object labels and collectors' auction catalogs, without valuation claims. No live filming or subtitles. Every car, person, event, brand and archival prop is fictional; remove real logos. Product views illustrate only the brief's existing features.

## WHY IT HOLDS

- **Past 3 seconds:** A specific part threatens an otherwise polished show entry.
- **Past 10 seconds:** The apparently helpful install photo answers the wrong question.
- **To the end:** The casting photo and catalog create a tangible resolution without promising a favorable judging result.
- **Comment trigger:** Enthusiasts can name the details they scrutinize; no request to identify or accuse a real owner.
