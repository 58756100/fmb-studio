# LA-05

**AUDIENCE:** Garages  
**TITLE:** The Same Puddle, Two Different Questions  
**LENGTH:** 50 seconds. The returning symptom, competing assumptions and limited but useful record need a full setup and payoff. Conversational pace is approximately 2.5 words per second.

## PREMISE

A customer's wet passenger footwell returns after a roof-seal repair, and everybody starts guessing whether the seal or installation failed.  
The original work photos recover what was actually done, giving the shop a shared starting point for a fresh inspection rather than a verdict.

## VISUAL STYLE

Vertical 9:16; rain blue, graphite and small amber highlights. Plain sans-serif headlines with hand-drawn circles around physical evidence. A photographic puddle becomes a lens into the earlier job. Mood: calm, accountable, mildly tense. Reference: a technician's annotated contact sheet, not courtroom or blame imagery. No live filming or subtitles. All cars, parts, people and shops are fictional; remove real logos. App views are illustrative and limited to supplied features.

## WHY IT HOLDS

- **Past 3 seconds:** The wet footwell makes the comeback tangible before any product appears.
- **Past 10 seconds:** The apparent two-answer problem opens into a better question: what work is actually documented?
- **To the end:** The audience sees how a record changes the conversation while inspection remains necessary; there is no invented diagnosis.
- **Comment trigger:** Garages can name the missing detail that usually slows a comeback. This is not a returned-part story or an exhaust support-ticket solution.
