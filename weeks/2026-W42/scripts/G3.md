# G3 — Any update on my car?

- **Audience:** garages
- **Format:** POV skit (two voices: customers and Rita)
- **Runtime target:** 27 s (hook 0–2.5 s, body 2.5–25 s, CTA 25–27 s)
- **Rhythm / device:** comic escalation. A buzz counter (1 → 9) in the headline band climbs with every text, while a wall clock and a half-done Tacoma show the real cost. Ping-pong lines get shorter as Rita's patience does, then everything goes silent.
- **Learning applied:** named character in the first 5 s; dialogue (two voices) test, garage arm.
- **Characters:** Rita and her customers are fictional. End card carries "Illustrative story".
- **Casting note:** Rita needs a female voice. Wayne is adding new voices, so cast Rita from that batch (audition on the hook B line plus "No. Watch this."). **Fallback if none is ready by render:** rename the character Ron and cast Eric; no line changes needed.

## 1. Story spine
1. **Who:** Rita, owner of a two-bay shop, under a grey Tacoma with the brakes half done.
2. **Wants:** to finish the Tacoma by 3 p.m. like she promised.
3. **But:** her phone buzzes "any update?" again and again from three customers. Every reply means gloves off, and the Tacoma doesn't move for an hour.
4. **Therefore:** on buzz eight she stops answering one by one. She posts a dated photo update to each car's page as she works and sends each customer their link: a live progress page (confirm). Built from live features, it's the car's public build page with dated log entries and photos (FMB shown working, 15.5–21 s = 57–78 %, landing at 15.5 s).
5. **Payoff + loop:** buzz nine is "Oh nice, the pads are on. No rush." Then silence. The Tacoma is done at 2:51. Then one more buzz: "Hey, can you do my brakes next?" That buzz is the first frame.

## 4. Voice direction
- **Rita (new female voice, see the casting note):** dry, tired, funny, never angry. ~150 wpm. Her lines get shorter as the counter climbs ("Still on the lift" → "Waiting on a part" → "Eleven minutes" → "No.").
- **Chris = all customers:** bright, oblivious, friendly. Vary the delivery per car (Miata guy upbeat, Civic customer apologetic, Tacoma owner relaxed) with ±5 % speed and style 0.15 → 0.3.
- **Emphasis words:** part · eleven minutes · hour · Watch this · next.
- **Gaps:** `tts.py --dialogue`. Customer → Rita gaps are tight (120 ms) to sell the ping-pong. The silence at 21–23 s is scripted and isn't tightened.
- *Italics = emphasis direction only; strip before TTS.*

## 7. Motion notes
1. **Buzz counter (whole film).** A rolling-number in the headline band, red while climbing, grey at 9 once it's a happy buzz. Each tick gets its own buzz sfx and a 2-frame phone shake (house rule: every action has a sound).
2. **Stacking threads (2.5–14 s).** Each new customer's imessage-chat-flow slides over the last as a stacked iOS 26 glass card. The stack depth itself shows the overload.
3. **Pull-back reveal (11–14 s).** The camera pulls back from the phone to show the stalled Tacoma, the wall clock rolling an hour, and the "Due 3 pm" sticky. It's the one wide shot before the turn.
4. **Triple-link rhythm (18.5–21 s).** Three share-sheet sends cut on the beat ("Link. Link. Link."), each with a whoosh. Use a whip-pan between threads, all landing on link previews with car cutouts.
5. **Silence + loop (21–25 s).** Hard audio cut to room tone, the clock jumps to 2:51, the Tacoma lowers on a soft spring, then the single buzz mirrors frame 0's position and shake exactly.
