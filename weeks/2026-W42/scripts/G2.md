# G2 — Anyone can post a clean car

- **Audience:** garages
- **Format:** before / after (mirrored split screen; the same question runs twice, then splits)
- **Runtime target:** 26 s (hook 0–2.5 s, body 2.5–24 s, CTA 24–26 s)
- **Rhythm / device:** symmetry, then rupture. For 11 s, both halves of the screen do exactly the same thing on the same frame (a "spot the difference" game with no difference). Then one side breaks the pattern. The viewer stays to find the crack.
- **Learning applied:** named character + car within 5 s.
- **Characters:** Jen, "Redline Works" and "Halvorsen Auto" are fictional. End card carries "Illustrative story".

## 1. Story spine
1. **Who:** Jen, about to hand her green E30 to a stranger for a $9,000 engine swap.
2. **Wants:** the shop that won't wreck her car.
3. **But:** two shops have identical feeds (same clean cars, same light) and send the identical DM reply, "We do these all the time." She can't tell them apart, so she asks both for one thing: "Show me one you've done."
4. **Therefore:** shop one sends three more beauty shots of other cars. Shop two sends one link: an E30 swap build page, every part with brand, dated log entries, photos on their lift, the shop's name on every entry (FMB shown working, 14–22 s = 54–85 %, landing at 14.5 s).
5. **Payoff + loop:** Jen books shop two. Shop one's DM sits on "Seen." Then both feeds snap back to identical, and the voice says "Same feed. Different proof." The last frame matches frame 0.

## 4. Voice direction
- **Eric** narrates: crisp, observational, slightly amused, ~160 wpm. It's the fastest read of the week, to match the split-screen tempo.
- **Emphasis words:** apart · We do these all the time (read flat, as a quote) · one · Not an E30 · link · dated · their · Seen.
- **Pauses:** short (0.2–0.3 s) everywhere. The only long beat is 0.4 s before "Shop one's still on 'Seen.'"
- One narrator, no dialogue. The rhythm comes from the picture's symmetry, not from voices.
- *Italics = emphasis direction only; strip before TTS.*

## 7. Motion notes
1. **Frame-locked symmetry (0–11 s).** Build the left and right phones as one component instanced twice. Every animation fires on the identical frame, including sound (doubled, panned L/R). It should feel almost wrong.
2. **The crack (11 s).** The first asymmetric event: the left phone gets new photos, the right freezes. Add a subtle 1-frame chromatic offset on the right phone only (rgb-glitch-text on "Delivered") as the visual "something's different".
3. **Whip-pan → collapse (14–15.5 s).** whip-pan right, then the split seam slides off-screen as the build page takes the full frame. It's the biggest layout change of the film, timed to the turn.
4. **Rolodex parts (15.5–19 s).** rolodex-flip for parts rows, each one landing with a soft click, dates in #86868B. The red check (19–22 s) is the only accent on screen.
5. **Snap-back loop (23.5 s).** The split returns and both grids snap to the exact frame-0 layout. Jen's E30 avatar is now on the right side of the seam: the only visible difference, which rewards a replay.
